## Summary

Non-Normal Distribution is a single U-Net that simultaneously denoises 28x28 grayscale images
corrupted with either Gaussian or uniform noise, classifies which distribution was used, and
estimates the Gaussian noise parameters. Built solo, September 2026.

## Context and constraints

The task required one shared representation to serve two very different objectives at once: a
dense, pixel-level reconstruction (denoising) and a coarse, global classification/estimation
task (identifying the noise source). The final model also had to run inference on a hidden test
set within a 5-minute GPU budget with no internet access, which ruled out large architectures
or heavy post-processing.

## Approach

Built a compact U-Net with a shared 2-stage encoder, a bottleneck, and a 2-stage decoder with
skip connections that produces the denoised image. A lightweight MLP classification head
branches off the bottleneck features to predict the noise type. Rather than adding a fourth
learned head for the Gaussian parameters, `mu` and `sigma` are recovered analytically as the
mean and standard deviation of the residual between the noisy input and the denoised output —
avoiding an extra learned output for a quantity the denoising task already implies. Trained
with a combined MSE (reconstruction) + BCE (classification) loss using Adam at a 1e-3 learning
rate, batch size 64, for 15 epochs, with a fixed seed and deterministic cuDNN settings for
reproducibility.

## Evaluation

Scored against the competition's four weighted sub-scores (25 points each): classification
accuracy scaled between 50% (chance) and 95%, reconstruction quality via PSNR scaled between 10
and 16 dB, and Gaussian `mu`/`sigma` estimation scored as full marks below a 0.005 MSE threshold
or zero above it.

## Result

Validation-set performance: 29.28 dB PSNR, 100% noise-type classification accuracy, 0.001356 MSE
on `mu`, and 0.001197 MSE on `sigma` — full marks on all four sub-scores, for a final score of
100/100.

## What changed

Estimating the Gaussian parameters analytically from the denoising residual, instead of training
a dedicated regression head for them, turned out to be enough to hit the accuracy threshold
without adding a fourth loss term or extra model capacity to tune.
